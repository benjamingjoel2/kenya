The Great Migration is not a single event but a continuous, year-round journey. Roughly 1.3 to 1.5 million wildebeest, accompanied by some 200,000 to 300,000 zebra and large numbers of Thomson's and Grant's gazelle, follow the rains in a vast clockwise loop through Tanzania's Serengeti and Kenya's [Masai Mara](/destinations/masai-mara).

Because the herds respond to rainfall and fresh grass rather than a calendar, no two years are identical. But the broad pattern is reliable, and understanding it is the key to choosing when, and where, to travel.

## The cycle in brief

- **January–March:** calving on the short-grass plains of the southern Serengeti.
- **April–May:** the long rains; herds move north and west through the central Serengeti.
- **June:** the rut and the Grumeti River crossings in the Serengeti's Western Corridor.
- **July:** the herds reach the northern Serengeti and begin entering the Mara.
- **August–September:** peak season in the Mara, with frequent crossings of the Mara River.
- **October:** herds disperse through the Mara and start to drift south.
- **November–December:** the short rains pull the herds back into the Serengeti.

## Month by month

### January to March: the calving season

After the short rains, the herds gather on the nutrient-rich volcanic grasslands around Ndutu and the southern Serengeti. In a window of about three weeks, usually peaking in February, around 400,000 calves are born, sometimes 8,000 in a single day.

Predators follow. This is one of the most dramatic times for cheetah, lion and hyena action, but it all happens in Tanzania. In the Mara, January to March is a lovely, quieter season: dry, green and excellent for resident wildlife, with lower rates in many camps.

### April and May: the long rains

The long rains usually arrive in late March or April and last into May. The herds begin moving north-west as the southern plains dry. This is the low season across East Africa. Some camps close; those that remain open offer considerable savings, lush landscapes and very few vehicles.

In the Mara, rain tends to fall in afternoon storms rather than all day, but some tracks become difficult. We often recommend this period to experienced travellers and photographers who appreciate dramatic skies.

### June: the Western Corridor

By June the herds are typically in the Serengeti's Western Corridor, where the rut is underway and the first serious river obstacle, the Grumeti, must be crossed. Its crocodiles are famously large. Towards the end of the month, the leading columns push north.

### July: arrival in the Mara

Usually from mid-to-late July, though it can be earlier or later, the first herds cross from the northern Serengeti into the Mara. The grass is long after the rains, and the arrival can be sudden: plains that were quiet one week fill with wildebeest the next.

### August and September: the river crossings

This is peak migration season in Kenya. The herds spread across the Mara and its conservancies, and the famous crossings of the Mara River, and its tributary the Talek, happen repeatedly as the animals move back and forth in search of grazing.

> A crossing is unpredictable. Herds may gather on the bank for hours and then turn away. Another group may plunge in without warning. Patience, good positioning and an experienced guide make all the difference.

Crossing points are well known to guides, and you will spend long mornings parked a respectful distance from the bank, watching the build-up. When it happens, the noise, dust and chaos are unforgettable. Nile crocodiles wait in the water; lion and leopard often hunt on the far bank.

### October: dispersal

Crossings continue in October, though usually less frequently, and the herds become more scattered. With fewer visitors after the August holidays, October can be an excellent compromise: good migration chances, rates beginning to drop, and the Mara's resident predators as active as ever.

### November and December: the short rains

The short rains, typically in November, draw the herds back south into the Serengeti. By December they are moving towards the calving grounds once more, and the cycle begins again. In the Mara, these are green, peaceful months with some of the year's best value, especially before the Christmas holidays.

## Is the migration worth chasing?

It depends on what you want. If seeing a river crossing is your dream, you need to be in the Mara between mid-July and October, ideally for at least four nights, with flexibility to spend full days at the river.

But the Mara is not empty outside migration season. It has one of the highest densities of resident lions in Africa, along with cheetah, leopard, elephant and buffalo. Many of our most experienced guides prefer the months either side of the migration, when sightings are just as good and far less busy.

## Where to stay

The northern Mara and the Mara Triangle (on the western side of the river) are generally best placed for crossings, while the private conservancies along the reserve's northern edge offer intimacy and excellent general game. A good approach is to combine both, or to stay in a conservancy with easy access to the river. Our [reserve vs conservancies guide](/blog/masai-mara-reserve-vs-private-conservancies) explains the trade-offs.

In peak season, the best camps book out 12 to 18 months in advance, particularly for August. If your dates are fixed, plan early.

## Making the most of it

- **Allow time.** Four nights is a good minimum; six gives you a real chance at multiple crossings.
- **Go private.** A private vehicle lets you wait at the river all day without compromising anyone else's plans. Our [Great Migration Special](/safaris/great-migration-special) is built around this.
- **Take to the air.** A [hot-air balloon flight](/experiences/hot-air-balloon-safaris) over the herds at dawn is one of the great safari experiences.
- **Bring the right kit.** A long lens (at least 300mm), a bean bag for support and a spare battery or two.
- **Remember the fees.** Masai Mara reserve fees are higher in the high season, currently around US$200 per adult per day from July to December, and are usually included in your trip price.

## Our view

The best time to see the Great Migration in Kenya is mid-July to October, with August and September the most reliable months for river crossings.
