Kenya is a country of extraordinary range. In a single trip you can watch lions on rolling grassland, track rhino on a high plateau beneath a snow-capped equatorial mountain, and finish with your feet in the warm Indian Ocean. The challenge is not finding somewhere wonderful, but choosing which wonderful places fit your time, your budget and the kind of experience you are hoping for.

This is our honest guide to the regions we send travellers to most, and why.

## The Masai Mara: the essential safari

If you only visit one place in Kenya, it should almost certainly be the [Masai Mara](/destinations/masai-mara). The reserve covers around 1,510 square kilometres of south-western Kenya, running into Tanzania's Serengeti, and is surrounded by a ring of private conservancies that roughly double the protected area.

What makes the Mara special is density. The open grassland supports remarkable numbers of resident lion, cheetah, leopard, hyena, elephant and buffalo, and visibility is superb. Between roughly July and October the [Great Migration](/experiences/great-migration) arrives from the Serengeti, adding well over a million wildebeest and hundreds of thousands of zebra to the mix.

- **Getting there:** 45 minutes to an hour by light aircraft from Nairobi's Wilson Airport.
- **Best for:** big cats, first-time safaris, the migration, photography.
- **Stay:** in a [private conservancy](/experiences/private-conservancies) if you can, for fewer vehicles, walks and night drives.

## Amboseli: elephants and Kilimanjaro

[Amboseli](/destinations/amboseli) is a small park, just 392 square kilometres, but it delivers one of Africa's most famous views: elephant herds crossing the dust pans with Kilimanjaro rising behind them. The mountain sits across the border in Tanzania and is often clearest at dawn and just before sunset.

Amboseli's elephants are among the most studied in the world, thanks to the Amboseli Trust for Elephants, which has followed individual families here since 1972. Large-tusked bulls are still seen, a rarity elsewhere in Africa. Spring-fed swamps keep the park green even in the dry season, drawing hippo, buffalo and abundant birdlife.

- **Getting there:** about an hour by light aircraft from Nairobi, or four to five hours by road.
- **Best for:** elephants, photography, a striking contrast to the Mara.

## Samburu and the north: a different Kenya

Fly an hour north of Nairobi and the landscape changes entirely. [Samburu](/destinations/samburu) is hot, arid and beautiful, with the Ewaso Nyiro river cutting through red earth and doum palms. Here you find the so-called Samburu Special Five: Grevy's zebra, reticulated giraffe, gerenuk, beisa oryx and Somali ostrich, none of which live in the south.

The Samburu people, close relatives of the Maasai, still move their livestock across this country, and cultural visits here tend to feel less rehearsed than elsewhere. Neighbouring [Meru](/destinations/meru) offers wild, empty bush and the story of Elsa the lioness from *Born Free*.

## Laikipia and Lewa: conservation country

The [Laikipia plateau](/destinations/laikipia-ol-pejeta) sits at around 1,800 metres between Mount Kenya and the Rift Valley, a patchwork of ranches and community conservancies that together hold some of Kenya's most important wildlife populations. [Ol Pejeta](/destinations/laikipia-ol-pejeta) is home to the largest black rhino sanctuary in East Africa and to Najin and Fatu, the last two northern white rhinos on Earth.

Next door, [Lewa](/destinations/lewa) is a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the continent's great conservation success stories, with healthy numbers of both black and white rhino.

What Laikipia offers above all is variety. Alongside game drives you can [track rhino on foot](/experiences/rhino-tracking), [ride horses](/experiences/horseback-safaris) among zebra, walk with camels or fly over the plateau by helicopter. It is our first choice for second-time visitors and [families](/experiences/family-safaris).

## The Rift Valley lakes

[Lake Naivasha](/destinations/naivasha-hells-gate), a two-hour drive from Nairobi, is a freshwater lake fringed by yellow fever trees and home to hippo and fish eagles. Nearby Hell's Gate National Park is one of the few parks where you can cycle or walk among zebra, giraffe and buffalo. [Lake Nakuru](/destinations/lake-nakuru) is known for its rhino sanctuary and, when water levels suit, its flamingos.

These are best used as a gentle first stop or a family-friendly break rather than the centrepiece of a trip.

## Tsavo: space and solitude

Together, [Tsavo](/destinations/tsavo) East and West cover more than 20,000 square kilometres, making them one of the largest protected areas in Africa. The elephants here are famously dusted red by the soil. Game is more scattered than in the Mara, but you will rarely share a sighting. Tsavo works well on the way to or from the coast.

## Mount Kenya

At 5,199 metres, [Mount Kenya](/destinations/mount-kenya) is Africa's second-highest mountain. The summit of Batian requires technical climbing, but Point Lenana (4,985 metres) is reachable by fit trekkers in four to five days. See our [Mount Kenya trekking](/experiences/mount-kenya-trekking) page for routes.

## The coast: Diani, Watamu and Lamu

Kenya's Indian Ocean coast is the natural end to a safari, and each beach has its own character:

- **[Diani](/destinations/diani-beach)** offers a long stretch of white sand south of Mombasa, with excellent snorkelling and kitesurfing. It is the easiest to reach and suits families.
- **[Watamu](/destinations/watamu-malindi)** is smaller and quieter, set within a marine national park with good diving and nesting turtles.
- **[Lamu](/destinations/lamu)** is something else entirely: a UNESCO-listed Swahili town with no cars, dhows in the harbour and a slow, romantic rhythm. Ideal for [honeymoons](/experiences/honeymoon-safaris).

## How to combine them

For a first safari of a week to ten days, we usually suggest two safari areas with contrasting landscapes, perhaps followed by the coast:

1. **Seven nights:** Masai Mara and Amboseli, or Masai Mara and Laikipia.
2. **Ten nights:** Masai Mara and Laikipia, with Samburu if you love wild landscapes.
3. **Twelve to fourteen nights:** two or three safari areas, then four or five nights at the coast.

> Three or four nights in each safari area is the sweet spot. Fewer and you spend too much of your trip travelling; more and you may prefer to add a contrasting region.

Light-aircraft flights link almost everywhere through Nairobi's Wilson Airport, so routing is rarely a problem, though flights between safari areas often require a connection.

## Our view

There is no single best of Kenya, only the best for you. The Mara is the unmissable heart, but the country's real magic often lies in the combination: the cats of the plains, the strange beauty of the north, the conservation stories of Laikipia, and the soft Swahili evenings of the coast.
