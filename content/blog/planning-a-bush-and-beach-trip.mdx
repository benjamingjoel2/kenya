After the early starts and dusty tracks of safari, there are few better ways to end a Kenyan journey than with a few days on the Indian Ocean. The water is warm, the reefs are healthy and Swahili culture adds a richness that sets the coast apart from a standard beach holiday.

Kenya's coast stretches for some 500 kilometres, from the Tanzanian border to Somalia. For [bush and beach](/experiences/bush-and-beach) trips, three destinations stand out: Diani, Watamu and Lamu. Each suits a different kind of traveller.

## Diani: easy, sandy and family-friendly

[Diani Beach](/destinations/diani-beach), around 30 kilometres south of Mombasa, is a 17-kilometre stretch of fine white sand protected by an offshore reef. At low tide, the lagoon becomes a series of calm, warm pools; at high tide, it is perfect for swimming.

### Why choose Diani

- **Access.** The Ukunda airstrip sits right behind the beach, and the drive from Mombasa's international airport is now quicker thanks to the Dongo Kundu bypass.
- **Activities.** Snorkelling and dolphin trips at Kisite-Mpunguti Marine Park, diving, kitesurfing (especially from June to September and January to March, when the monsoon winds blow) and deep-sea fishing.
- **Families.** Gentle water, a wide range of villas and easy logistics.
- **Nature.** The Kaya Kinondo sacred forest and the Colobus Conservation centre, home to the rare Angolan colobus monkey.

Diani is the most developed of the three, with restaurants and beach bars along the main road. Choose your villa carefully for privacy.

## Watamu: reef, turtles and quiet

[Watamu](/destinations/watamu-malindi), about 25 minutes south of Malindi, is smaller and more relaxed. Its coves and coral outcrops lie within the Watamu Marine National Park, part of a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

### Why choose Watamu

- **Marine life.** Some of the best snorkelling and diving on the coast, with green turtles nesting on the beaches and whale sharks occasionally passing between October and March.
- **Arabuko-Sokoke Forest.** The largest remaining coastal forest in East Africa, home to rare birds such as the Sokoke scops owl.
- **Gede Ruins.** An atmospheric 13th-century Swahili town hidden in the forest.
- **Character.** Watamu has a smaller, village feel, with a strong Italian influence in its restaurants.

## Lamu: Swahili culture and romance

[Lamu](/destinations/lamu) is not a beach resort but an archipelago of islands steeped in more than 700 years of Swahili history. Lamu Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site: narrow alleyways, carved wooden doors and coral-stone houses, with donkeys and dhows rather than cars.

Most visitors stay in Shela, a village a short boat ride or 45-minute walk from Lamu town, where restored Swahili houses open onto a 12-kilometre beach of dunes and empty sand.

### Why choose Lamu

- **Atmosphere.** There is nowhere else quite like it in East Africa: unhurried, authentic and deeply romantic.
- **Dhow sailing.** Sunset sails, island picnics and seafood barbecues on [traditional dhows](/experiences/lamu-dhow-sailing).
- **Honeymoons.** Its seclusion makes it a favourite for [honeymooners](/experiences/honeymoon-safaris).

Lamu is less suited to young children who want shallow lagoons and kids' clubs, and the snorkelling is not as good as in Diani or Watamu.

## Getting there: flights from the Mara and Nairobi

The coast is easy to reach by scheduled light aircraft and domestic flights.

- **Diani (Ukunda):** Around 1 hour 15 minutes from Nairobi Wilson. Seasonal direct flights also link the Masai Mara and Ukunda, usually with one stop, in around two hours.
- **Watamu (Malindi):** Fly into Malindi, about 1 hour 15 minutes from Nairobi. Scheduled jets also connect from Jomo Kenyatta International. Watamu is a 25-minute drive.
- **Lamu (Manda):** Manda Island airport is around 1 hour 30 minutes to 1 hour 45 minutes from Nairobi Wilson, with a short boat transfer across to Lamu or Shela.

From the Mara, you will often connect through Wilson, which can be done on the same day with a little careful timing. Going home, you can fly back to Nairobi for an evening international departure, or connect via Mombasa.

## Luggage on light aircraft

Most safari flights use small Cessna Caravans, and the luggage limit is strict: typically **15 kg per person, including hand luggage, in soft-sided bags** without wheels or a rigid frame. Bags must fit into the aircraft's small hold.

A few practical tips:

- Pack a single soft duffel of around 60 x 30 x 25 cm.
- Most camps offer laundry, so you need fewer clothes than you think.
- Excess luggage can usually be stored in Nairobi and collected on your way home, or sent ahead.
- If you need more allowance, for example for camera equipment, an extra seat can be bought.

## Best months for the coast

Kenya's coast is warm year-round, with sea temperatures from about 25°C to 29°C, but the seasons matter:

- **December to March:** hot, humid and generally calm, with the northeast monsoon (*kaskazi*) bringing clear water and excellent snorkelling. Peak season.
- **April to June:** the long rains, heaviest in May. Many Lamu houses close in May and June, and seas can be rough.
- **July to October:** cooler and breezier under the southeast monsoon (*kusi*), with occasional showers. Lovely weather for exploring, and pairs well with the [Great Migration](/experiences/great-migration) in the Mara.
- **November:** the short rains, usually brief, with good value.

## How many nights?

For most travellers, **four to five nights** is the right length at the coast. Three nights can feel rushed after the travel day, while a week suits those who truly want to unwind.

Here is a simple guide:

1. **Seven to nine nights in total:** four to five on safari, three to four at the coast.
2. **Ten to twelve nights:** five to seven on safari across two regions, then four or five at the beach.
3. **Two weeks or more:** two or three safari regions and five or six nights at the coast.

> Always do the safari first and the beach second. You will want to rest after the early starts, and it is far easier to go home relaxed.

## Choosing between them

- **Choose Diani** for families, easy logistics, beach activities and a classic resort feel.
- **Choose Watamu** for marine life, diving and a quieter, village atmosphere.
- **Choose Lamu** for culture, romance and complete escape.
